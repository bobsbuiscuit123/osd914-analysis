Abstract
Cross-tissue physiological stress modeling remains a profound challenge in aerospace medicine, where traditional
transcriptomic analyses evaluate microgravity adaptations within isolated organ silos. This tissue-specific insulation intro-
duces a critical blind spot, potentially obscuring systemic multi-organ signaling correlations that impact astronaut health
during extended space missions. To address this systemic knowledge gap, we introduce a deep learning pipeline to model
and map predictive cross-tissue associations along a candidate liver-brain microRNA (miRNA) co-expression axis under
conditions of microgravity. Utilizing synchronized miRNA expression matrices derived from female BALB/cAnNTac
mice flown on-board the International Space Station during the Rodent Research Reference Mission-1 (RRRM-1 / RR-8)
payload (n = 9; GEO Accession: GSE294046), we engineered a customized PyTorch Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP)
optimized via Adam and regularized using Dropout (p = 0.3) and L2 weight decay (λ = 10−2). Input features undergo a
nested log1p transform followed by standard Z-score scaling, and the network does not employ fixed initialization seeds,
reflecting the stochastic nature of its optimization. The pipeline operates on a preselected high-confidence signature
of 6 peripheral liver miRNA inputs and 5 central brain miRNA targets. Model evaluation was executed via rigorous
Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV), coupled with a feature tracking layer to decode input contributions to
cross-tissue predictions.
Our computational framework successfully mapped a candidate predictive cross-tissue miRNA co-expression axis,
achieving an informative LOOCV validation mean squared error (VMSE) of 1.1062 as a predictive baseline for candidate
multi-tissue miRNA associations. Feature tracking analysis identified mmu-miR-17-5p as the dominant peripheral
liver anchor driving the cross-tissue predictive mapping, with mmu-miR-122-5p ranking third in relative attribution
weight. Downstream systems biology analysis revealed a complex, divergent regulatory landscape rather than a uniform
suppression pattern: central brain mmu-miR-124-3p was observed at higher levels in flight samples relative to habitat and
vivarium ground controls, while let-7 family targets exhibited a heterogeneous, mixed expression profile across the flight
cohort.
These findings suggest the empirical viability of decoding non-linear, multi-tissue miRNA co-expression trends from
high-dimensional, sparse spaceflight datasets using regularized deep neural network architectures. By characterizing this
candidate liver-brain miRNA axis, this study highlights systemic physiological correlations missed by traditional single-
tissue workflows. Ultimately, this multi-organ modeling framework establishes a predictive computational foundation for
targeted, multi-tissue biomedical hypothesis generation designed to safeguard neurological health and mitigate systemic
decay in astronauts during long-duration interplanetary missions.
Keywords: spaceflight biology, miRNA co-expression axis, cross-tissue dependencies, neural networks, microgravity
stress, feature attribution, mmu-miR-17-5p, predictive modeling.
1. Introduction
Spaceflight exposes biological organisms to a severe, in-
terconnected matrix of physical and physiological stres-
sors. These environmental challenges include sustained
microgravity, cosmic ionizing radiation, chronic circa-
dian disruption, hyper-confinement, altered nutritional
paradigms, and acute psychological stress profiles asso-
ciated with mission execution. As humanity transitions
toward long-duration deep space exploration—including
extended lunar habitability and crewed Martian transits—
characterizing the systemic cellular adaptations driven by
these stressors becomes an urgent priority for aerospace
medicine. Exposure to the spaceflight environment is as-
sociated with sweeping, systemic molecular shifts across
diverse regulatory networks. However, tracking these
systemic adjustments remains difficult because contem-
porary aerospace research heavily relies on single-tissue
paradigms that isolate physiological systems into inde-

pendent analytical silos.
The liver represents a critical systemic sensor and pri-
mary metabolic hub during environmental adaptation. Ter-
restrial research establishes the liver as the central gate-
keeper for metabolic regulation, foreign compound detox-
ification, systemic redox balance, innate immune signal-
ing, and macro-nutrient energy homeostasis. Under space-
flight conditions, transcriptomic assays frequently reveal
substantial perturbations within hepatic tissue. Prior
spaceflight investigations have documented notable shifts
in hepatic lipid metabolism, altered mitochondrial oxida-
tive phosphorylation efficiency, systemic inflammatory
cascades, and shifts in endogenous antioxidant machinery.
These expression patterns suggest that the liver may act as
a high-fidelity peripheral sensor, translating external envi-
ronmental spaceflight stressors into systemic molecular
signals.
Simultaneously, the central nervous system (CNS) ex-
periences significant cellular and structural changes dur-
ing spaceflight. Key responses recorded in rodent and
human crew cohorts include marked neuroinflammatory
signatures, altered neurotransmitter clearance rates, mi-
crostructural white matter shifts, and disruptions to cir-
cadian behavioral regularities. Both animal models and
human neuroimaging confirm that the brain adapts dy-
namically to microgravity conditions. Yet, despite paral-
lel observations of hepatic metabolic stress and central
neuroinflammation, the aerospace community tradition-
ally analyzes liver and brain datasets independently. This
conventional organ-specific workflow risks overlooking
complex, coordinated co-expression patterns occurring
between peripheral organs and the central nervous sys-
tem. Terrestrial clinical studies suggest that liver-brain
cross-talk may involve complex communication pathways,
including circulating metabolic substrates, endocrine sig-
naling, inflammatory cytokines, extracellular vesicles, and
regulatory non-coding RNAs.
Consequently, a major knowledge gap remains regard-
ing the molecular associations through which spaceflight-
induced hepatic stress may co-vary with cellular and func-
tional states within the CNS. Characterizing a candidate
liver-brain miRNA co-expression axis is essential for a
more complete, unified understanding of systemic space-
flight adaptation, and it could highlight uncharacterized
correlations affecting astronaut health. Traditional differ-
ential expression analysis workflows struggle to capture
these multi-tissue associations due to high dimensional-
ity, inherent noise, and the small sample sizes typical of
spaceflight datasets.
To address these computational constraints, we present
a deep learning framework designed to map predictive
cross-tissue miRNA co-expression associations along a
candidate spaceflight-induced liver-brain axis. Moving
away from traditional single-tissue analyses, we imple-
mented a customized PyTorch Multi-Layer Perceptron
(MLP) architecture to model the relationship between
a preselected signature of 6 peripheral liver miRNA in-
puts and 5 central brain miRNA targets. The pipeline
was applied to synchronized miRNA expression matrices
from female BALB/cAnNTac mice flown on-board the
International Space Station during the Rodent Research
Reference Mission-1 (RRRM-1 / RR-8; GEO Accession:
GSE294046), mapping predictive cross-tissue associa-
tions with constrained, regularized modeling.
Our framework identified a candidate cross-tissue
miRNA co-expression pattern centered on the hepatic
feature mmu-miR-17-5p, which emerged as the domi-
nant peripheral predictive anchor in our feature tracking
analysis. Within the modeled cohort, this liver-profile
anchor co-occurs with a complex downstream regulatory
landscape in the brain: central mmu-miR-124-3p was ob-
served at higher levels in flight samples relative to ground
controls, while let-7 family targets displayed a hetero-
geneous, mixed response rather than a uniform trend.
This study demonstrates the utility of using regularized
neural networks to map complex multi-organ miRNA co-
expression patterns within spaceflight data. It provides a
concrete computational framework to move beyond iso-
lated tissue silos and accelerate the generation of targeted
biomedical hypotheses for long-duration space missions.
2. Materials and Methods
All pipelines were implemented using Python 3.10 and
PyTorch 2.1. Consistent with the stochastic nature of
the network’s optimization, no fixed initialization seeds
were enforced across execution runs; reported metrics
reflect representative performance under this stochastic
initialization regime.
2.1 Dataset and Data Preprocessing
The primary data utilized in this study were obtained from
the NASA Open Science Data Repository (OSDR), GEO
Accession GSE294046, targeting the miRNA expression
cohorts of the Rodent Research Reference Mission-1
(RRRM-1 / RR-8) spaceflight payload. The experimen-
tal population consisted of flight-synchronized female
BALB/cAnNTac mice flown on board the International
Space Station ( n = 9). Synchronized miRNA expres-
sion matrices were extracted across two primary tissue
types: peripheral hepatic (liver) tissue and central nervous
system (brain) cortical tissue.
The raw input matrices consisted of focused non-
coding RNA sequencing profiles, comprising 717 liver
miRNA features and 637 brain miRNA targets. From
these, a preselected high-confidence, biologically rele-
vant signature subset of 6 liver miRNA inputs and 5

2.3 Cross-Validation Framework
Evaluating deep architectures on sparse spaceflight
datasets requires robust validation protocols to avoid opti-
mistically biased performance metrics. Standard training-
validation splits are mathematically unviable when n = 9.

Validation (LOOCV) framework.

2.4 Feature Tracking and Attribution
To extract the input contributions governing the model’s
cross-tissue predictions and assess feature importance be-
yond the “black-box” nature of the MLP, we applied a
feature tracking layer across the constrained peripheral
hepatic miRNA input subset. Attribution scores were
calculated to quantify each input feature’s relative con-
tribution to predictive cross-tissue estimates across all
LOOCV folds. Attribution magnitudes were systemati-
cally ranked to identify the dominant peripheral miRNA
anchors driving the candidate cross-tissue liver-brain co-
expression axis.
3. Results
3.1 Cross-Tissue Model Performance
The regularized PyTorch Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP)
architecture was evaluated under a strict Leave-One-Out
Cross-Validation (LOOCV) loop to determine its capacity
to model predictive miRNA co-expression associations
across isolated tissue types. The deep learning framework
achieved a global cross-talk Validation Mean Squared
Error (VMSE) of 1.1062, serving as an informative pre-
dictive baseline for candidate multi-tissue miRNA associ-
ations under this constrained, small-n regime.

3.2 Identification of Peripheral Anchors via Feature
Tracking
To extract the input features governing the network’s
cross-tissue predictions, we evaluated the trained models
through the post-hoc feature tracking layer. Attribution
scores were computed across all 6 preselected peripheral
hepatic miRNA inputs.
Feature tracking analysis identified mmu-miR-17-5p
as the dominant peripheral anchor within the cross-tissue
predictive model. It consistently exhibited the highest rela-
tive attribution magnitude across all independent LOOCV
folds, establishing it as the primary candidate predictive
bridge associating peripheral liver miRNA expression
with downstream central nervous system miRNA vari-
ations. Notably, mmu-miR-122-5p, while biologically
relevant as a liver injury marker, ranked third in relative
attribution weight within this constrained feature subset.

3.3 Downstream Systems Biology Analysis
Downstream systems biology analysis was conducted to
contextualize the candidate miRNA co-expression asso-
ciations identified by the predictive model. Rather than
a uniform, idealized suppression pattern, the cleaned ex-
pression matrix reveals a complex, divergent regulatory
landscape across tissue compartments.
Within the hepatic matrix, the peripheral liver pro-
file anchored by mmu-miR-17-5p was associated with
a baseline homeostatic disruption pattern, consistent with
broader shifts in tissue metabolic balance under space-
flight stress. In the brain, central mmu-miR-124-3p was
observed at marked higher levels in flight samples rela-
tive to habitat and vivarium ground controls, indicating a
CNS microenvironment shift rather than the suppression
trend previously assumed. The let-7 family targets, in
contrast, exhibited a heterogeneous, mixed expression
profile across the flight cohort, without a single, synchro-
nized directional trend, suggesting complex regulatory
modulation rather than uniform down-regulation.

4. Discussion
Our computational experiments present empirical find-
ings in contemporary spaceflight biology, introducing
a constrained framework for mapping candidate multi-
organ miRNA co-expression associations under micro-
gravity. The Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) framework
demonstrated that regularized deep learning architectures
can successfully model predictive miRNA co-expression
trends across isolated tissue systems, achieving an infor-
mative validation mean squared error of 1.1062 under
LOOCV . The cross-validation analysis suggests that the
regularized neural network framework captures candi-
date cross-tissue co-expression signals from the focused,
sparse spaceflight signature without falling victim to the
severe overfitting that typically limits traditional linear
models in space biology, providing a useful predictive
reference point as additional spaceflight cohorts become
available.
4.1 Predictive Value Independent of Large Sample
Sizes
The core contribution of this work lies in providing a
predictive mapping of candidate cross-tissue miRNA as-
sociations using a sparse, focused spaceflight expression
signature. Traditionally, the aerospace community treats
individual tissues as isolated biological silos, analyzing

4.2 A Complex, Divergent Liver-Brain Regulatory
Landscape
It is critical to distinguish the localized tissue expres-
sion changes commonly reported in spaceflight literature
from the systemic co-expression correlations identified
by our pipeline. Our feature tracking analysis revealed
mmu-miR-17-5p, rather than mmu-miR-122-5p, as the
dominant peripheral predictive anchor—an important cor-
rection that reframes the candidate hepatic signature driv-
ing cross-tissue predictions. mmu-miR-122-5p remains
biologically relevant as a liver-specific marker but ranks
only third in relative predictive contribution within this
constrained model.
The downstream expression analysis likewise resists a
simplified, uniform narrative. Central mmu-miR-124-3p
was observed at higher levels in flight samples relative to
ground controls, the inverse of an idealized suppression
hypothesis, suggesting a genuine CNS microenvironment
shift rather than straightforward down-regulation. The let-
7 family, meanwhile, displayed a heterogeneous, mixed
response across the flight cohort rather than a single syn-
chronized trend. Taken together, these findings point to a
complex, divergent regulatory landscape across the liver-
brain axis, in which peripheral and central miRNA com-
partments shift in non-uniform, occasionally counterintu-
itive directions. This candidate cross-tissue co-expression
pattern suggests that peripheral organ miRNA shifts may
co-vary with the regulatory miRNA environment within
the central nervous system during long-duration missions,
though wet-lab validation is required to establish any

4.3 Implications for Multi-Organ Hypothesis Genera-
tion
If a spaceflight biological system exhibits candidate cross-
tissue miRNA co-expression dependencies, designing
countermeasures targeting isolated organs may be in-
sufficient. Monitoring central nervous system markers
without also tracking the underlying hepatic expression
environment may represent an incomplete observational
approach, as the peripheral liver miRNA profile may co-
vary with downstream CNS expression states in directions
that are not always intuitive.
Future biomedical hypotheses should enforce a multi-
organ observational approach. By utilizing this predictive
computational framework, aerospace medical researchers
can identify peripheral, accessible tissue miRNA pro-
files (such as mmu-miR-17-5p) as candidate indicators of
downstream central nervous system miRNA expression
trends. Importantly, the static co-expression associations
identified here represent candidate predictive correlations
and should not be interpreted as established physiological
mechanisms.
4.4 Limitations and Future Work
While our regularized framework offers a novel lens on
systemic spaceflight miRNA co-expression biology, sev-
eral distinct limitations must be acknowledged. First, the
RRRM-1 cohort is small ( n = 9), reflecting the strict
physical and logistical constraints of International Space
Station payloads. Replicating these candidate predictive
cross-tissue associations across larger, multi-modal hu-
man and animal datasets within the NASA Open Science
Data Repository will be essential to determine whether the
mmu-miR-17-5p liver-brain candidate axis generalizes
across diverse biological cohorts and longer spaceflight
durations.
Second, the current pipeline is strictly correlational: it
captures co-expression trends between peripheral liver
miRNA inputs and central brain miRNA targets within a
static cross-sectional dataset, and the network’s stochas-
tic initialization means reported metrics should be inter-
preted as representative rather than fully deterministic.
Future wet-lab validation—specifically utilizing targeted
miRNA knockdown models or localized hepatic antioxi-
dant interventions in simulated microgravity systems—is
required to determine whether any causal or mechanistic
relationship underlies the identified co-expression pat-
terns. Future work will also explore the integration of
multi-tissue variational autoencoders (β-V AEs) to explic-
itly separate task-specific environmental expression shifts
from baseline inter-subject physiological variance, along-
side repeated-seed robustness analyses to quantify the
variance attributable to stochastic initialization.
5. Conclusion
We present a constrained deep learning analysis of can-
didate cross-tissue miRNA co-expression associations in-
duced by the spaceflight environment. Through a regular-
ized PyTorch Multi-Layer Perceptron framework applied
to a focused signature of 6 peripheral liver and 5 cen-
tral brain miRNA features from female BALB/cAnNTac
mice (RRRM-1 / RR-8; GEO Accession: GSE294046),
we report empirical findings suggesting that traditional
single-tissue workflows may miss candidate system-wide
multi-organ miRNA co-expression patterns. Our compu-
tational framework mapped a candidate predictive cross-
tissue miRNA co-expression axis with an informative
LOOCV validation mean squared error of 1.1062, reveal-
ing a complex, divergent regulatory landscape in which
spaceflight-associated hepatic miRNA shifts—anchored
by mmu-miR-17-5p—co-occur with a marked upregula-
tion of central mmu-miR-124-3p and a heterogeneous,
mixed response across the let-7 family.
By establishing mmu-miR-17-5p as the primary candi-
date peripheral tracking anchor within this correlational
liver-brain axis, this study introduces an empirical pi-
lot tool for mapping predictive miRNA co-expression
trends within high-dimensional, sample-sparse spaceflight
data. Future aerospace biomedical research may benefit
from moving beyond isolated organ tracking and ideal-
ized uniform-suppression assumptions toward integrated,
multi-tissue miRNA profiling paradigms that embrace the
complexity of divergent regulatory responses. Monitor-
ing peripheral hepatic miRNA shifts as candidate indi-
cators of central nervous system expression states repre-
sents a promising hypothesis for future targeted investiga-
tion toward dependable, multi-organ biomedical strategies
to safeguard astronaut neurological health during long-
duration interplanetary missions.
